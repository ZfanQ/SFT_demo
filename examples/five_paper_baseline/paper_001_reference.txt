This retrospective single-center study evaluated whether AI-derived embryo assessments could predict in vitro fertilization outcomes. Researchers analyzed 300 treatment cycles from 2020 to 2024 and 742 embryos, combining time-lapse-derived viability scores with clinical and embryological characteristics. Prediction models were developed using a 70:30 training-test split and five-fold cross-validation within the training set. Higher AI scores were associated with better reproductive outcomes: clinical pregnancy rates ranged from 18.5% for scores below 55 to 74.0% for scores of at least 85. Maternal age, anti-Mullerian hormone, blastocyst quality, and AI score independently predicted clinical pregnancy. The reported predictive model achieved a ROC AUC of 0.89. These findings suggest potential value for AI-assisted embryo assessment, but the modest, retrospective cohort and absence of external validation limit generalizability. Prospective multicenter studies are needed to establish whether using the system improves clinical outcomes.
