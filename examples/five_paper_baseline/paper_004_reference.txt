This retrospective two-center study evaluated GPT-5-Thinking as a diagnostic assistant using MRI report text from 1,000 patients with pathologically confirmed orbital or head-and-neck tumors. The model received clinical context and imaging descriptions with diagnostic impressions removed, then produced benign-malignant classifications and ranked differential diagnoses. Its top-1 accuracy exceeded routine generalist performance and was numerically similar to subspecialist performance in both tumor groups. In a paired reader study involving seven generalists, model assistance was associated with higher top-1 accuracy: from 61.4% to 70.3% for orbital tumors and from 47.0% to 61.0% for head-and-neck tumors. The findings suggest that report-based reasoning may help narrow expertise gaps. However, performance depended on the completeness of source reports, and nonconsecutive sampling and nonstandardized use of assistance constrain interpretation. Prospective multicenter evaluation is needed to establish clinical utility.
