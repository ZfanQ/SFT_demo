This study investigated whether crime-prevention advisories reassure residents or instead heighten perceived insecurity, and how associations vary by location and age. A cross-sectional survey of 687 residents across 40 communities in Nanjing was analyzed using ordinal logistic regression with age-by-channel interactions. Advisories noticed at building entrances were associated with higher perceived safety, whereas those on community noticeboards and in elevator lobbies were associated with lower perceived safety. Age interactions indicated less favorable associations for television news among adults aged 31-40 and mobile applications among those aged 41-50, relative to residents over 50. The findings suggest that safety communication should account for spatial context and life stage rather than rely on uniform messaging. However, single-item safety measurement, unmeasured psychological mechanisms, possible reverse causality, and the single-city cross-sectional design limit causal interpretation and generalizability.
