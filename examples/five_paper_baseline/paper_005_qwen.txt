This study investigates how spatial context and age moderate the impact of safety advisories on residents' perception of safety in Nanjing, China. Using ordinal logistic regression, the research analyzes data from 687 residents, revealing that crime prevention tips at unit entrances significantly enhance safety perceptions (B = 0.370, p < 0.05), while reminders in elevator lobbies correspond to a sharp decline in security feelings (B = −0.741, p < 0.01). Age also plays a critical role, with early-to-middle-aged groups (31–40 and 41–50 years) showing a systematically lower likelihood of high safety alignment, particularly when exposed to Television News and Mobile Applications. The findings highlight the "Information Paradox," where safety advisories may inadvertently heighten fear rather than provide reassurance, depending on spatial and demographic factors. The study underscores the importance of tailoring communication strategies to spatial and demographic contexts to effectively mitigate unintended environmental anxiety and enhance public safety. Limitations include the study's focus on a single city and the potential for reverse causality in cross-sectional data.
