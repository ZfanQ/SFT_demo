This study investigates the role of N6-methyladenosine (m6A) in temozolomide (TMZ) resistance in glioblastoma (GBM) cells, using multiple GBM cell lines including U87 MG, LN-229, T98G, and U251 MG. The objective was to explore m6A's contribution to TMZ resistance, assess METTL3 inhibition effects, and understand the molecular mechanisms of m6A regulation of TMZ resistance-associated genes. Methods included generating TMZ-resistant (TMZ-R) cell lines, RNA sequencing, m6A immunoprecipitation, RNA stability assays, colony formation assays, cell viability assays, qPCR, and bisulfite sequencing. METTL3 inhibition with STM2457 reduced expression of TMZ resistance-associated genes, including MGMT, and suppressed GBM cell growth. IC50 values for STM2457 were 93.6 μM in parental U87 MG cells and 81.7 μM in TMZ-R cells. MGMT mRNA was stabilized by m6A methylation, with its half-life reduced from ~20 h to ~8 h upon STM2457 treatment. The study concludes that m6A methylation stabilizes key genes and pathways promoting TMZ resistance, and METTL3 inhibition can reverse resistance by destabilizing m6A-methylated transcripts. However, the study is limited to in vitro models, and the exact mechanisms of m6A stabilization and the full range of m6A-regulated pathways in TMZ resistance remain to be elucidated. Long-term effects of METTL3 inhibition on GBM progression or patient outcomes were not explored.
