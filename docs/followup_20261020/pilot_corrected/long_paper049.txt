This study investigated the role of the long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) PGM5P4-AS1 in breast cancer (BRCA) progression. Using data from three GEO datasets and bioinformatics analysis, PGM5P4-AS1 was found to be significantly downregulated in BRCA tumor tissues compared to normal tissues. Clinicopathological analysis revealed that low PGM5P4-AS1 expression was associated with poor differentiation, advanced tumor node metastasis (TNM) staging, and lymph node metastasis. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and multivariate Cox regression confirmed that low PGM5P4-AS1 expression independently predicted poor prognosis. Functional experiments using BRCA cell lines (SK-BR-3 and MDA-MB-231) showed that PGM5P4-AS1 overexpression suppressed cell proliferation, migration, and invasion while promoting apoptosis. Mechanistically, PGM5P4-AS1 acted as a molecular sponge for miR-3664-5p, which in turn targeted KLF9. miR-3664-5p overexpression reversed the suppressive effects of PGM5P4-AS1 on BRCA cell functions, and KLF9 silencing reversed the effects of miR-3664-5p downregulation. These findings suggest that PGM5P4-AS1 suppresses malignant breast cancer cell behavior through the miR-3664-5p/KLF9 axis. The study highlights PGM5P4-AS1 as a potential prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for BRCA. However, the study is limited by its retrospective design, small sample size, and lack of in vivo validation. Additionally, potential off-target effects of PGM5P4-AS1 and the need for further exploration of signaling pathways remain areas for future research.
