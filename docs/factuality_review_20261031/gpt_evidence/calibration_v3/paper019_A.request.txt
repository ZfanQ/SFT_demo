Indeed, this diagnostic workflow should be further validated on\na larger dataset, and we are currently planning a multicentre\nstudy to test its robustness and scalability. Furthermore, the\nuse of high throughput PromethION platform (ONT) will increase the coverage depth, related accuracy and precision.\nIn summary, the diagnostic pipeline based on both native\nDNA and long PCR sequencing allows to correctly detect\nand characterize STRs. Although NanoExpansion proved to\nbe a valuable component of the analysis pipeline, the current\nimplementation still has some limitations, which will be addressed in further development. First, its performance should\nbe validated on additional datasets, possibly generated in different experimental conditions, to assess the robustness and\ngeneralizability of the algorithm. This step is crucial before integrating NanoExpansion into routine clinical bioinformatic\nworkflows. Additional developments also include improved\nhandling of highly fragmented reads, and a more efficient automatic motif detection module, to enable the reveal of previously unreported interruption patterns. Despite these limitations, NanoExpansion provides a comprehensive framework\nfor the analysis and characterization of pathogenic repeat expansions and represents a promising foundation for future\nmethodological developments.\nFinally, the patients belonging to this research cohort were\nundergoing clinical examinations, magnetic resonance imaging, and neuropsychological evaluation. This is a crucial aspect, since collecting these different sources of information\nwould allow to correlate them with the genomic data. Integrating multi-modal data and exploiting statistical methods\nand machine learning algorithms would help to shed light on\nthe genotype–phenotype correlation, which remains an open\nquestion with regard to these REDs.\nThis study considered a relatively large cohort of repeat expansion patients, which proved the robustness of the proposed\ndiagnostic pipeline.
