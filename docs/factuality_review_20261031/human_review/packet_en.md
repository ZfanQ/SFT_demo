## paper019

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper019.pdf)

### Candidate A

This paper presents a study evaluating the use of nanopore sequencing combined with adaptive sampling and a novel tool called NanoExpansion to accurately characterize repeat expansion disorders. The objective was to improve the diagnostic accuracy and efficiency of identifying these disorders, which are caused by abnormal expansions of short tandem repeats (STRs). The study included 132 participants, including patients with myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1), DM2, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), and other neurodegenerative conditions, as well as healthy controls. Native DNA was sequenced using adaptive sampling on Oxford Nanopore platforms, and NanoExpansion was developed to analyze the data, identifying expanded alleles and detecting sequence interruptions. The results showed that nanopore sequencing with NanoExpansion accurately characterized all cases of DM1, DM2, ALS, Friedreich ataxia, Huntington’s disease, and Kennedy disease, and identified several cases of spinocerebellar ataxias (SCAs) and cerebellar ataxia with neuropathy and vestibular areflexia syndrome (CANVAS). Compared to long PCR, nanopore sequencing provided more accurate results for larger expansions, and detected repeat interruptions in a significant proportion of DM1 cases. However, the study noted limitations, including the potential for sequencing errors and the need for further validation on larger datasets. The findings suggest that the integration of nanopore sequencing and NanoExpansion offers a promising approach for the comprehensive characterization of repeat expansion disorders, with the potential to improve routine genetic diagnosis.


### Candidate B

This study evaluates the diagnostic utility of Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) long-read sequencing, combined with adaptive sampling (AS) and a novel Python-based tool, NanoExpansion, for characterizing repeat expansion disorders (REDs). The researchers analyzed native DNA and long-PCR products from 132 participants, including patients with myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1), myotonic dystrophy type 2 (DM2), C9orf72-associated amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), and various spinocerebellar ataxias (SCAs), alongside healthy controls. Results demonstrated that ONT sequencing accurately characterized all DM1, DM2, ALS, Friedreich ataxia, Huntington’s disease, and Kennedy disease cases. Specifically, the pipeline correctly identified expansions in 82% of DM1 subjects, with high correlation between native and long-PCR estimates for smaller expansions (≤800 repeats). The study also identified several SCA and CANVAS cases, though long-PCR showed poor agreement for CANVAS due to high GC content. While the "super-accurate" (sup) basecalling model significantly reduced errors compared to the "high-accuracy" (hac) model, the study was limited by a relatively small cohort size and the need for further multi-center validation. The authors conclude that this integrated pipeline provides a comprehensive, efficient framework for RED diagnosis.


## paper024

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper024.pdf)

### Candidate A

This study investigated the role of well-being comparisons in mental health outcomes following loss, using a two-wave longitudinal design with 315 bereaved individuals. The objective was to examine whether aversive well-being comparisons—such as social, temporal, counterfactual, and expectation-based comparisons—predicted higher psychopathology, including prolonged grief disorder (PGD), posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), depression, anxiety, somatic symptoms, and loneliness. The study also explored whether these comparisons mediated the relationship between comparison frequency and mental health symptoms. Participants completed assessments six months apart, evaluating well-being comparisons, worry, rumination, avoidance, childhood adversity, and mental health outcomes. Preregistered hypotheses were partially supported: higher frequency of aversive well-being comparisons, comparison discrepancy, and negative comparison affective impact predicted elevated psychopathology six months later. These effects remained significant after controlling for autoregressive pathways, worry, rumination, avoidance, and childhood adversity. Mediation analyses suggested that frequent comparisons amplified perceived discrepancies, leading to negative affective impact, which in turn contributed to depression, anxiety, and somatic symptoms. The study found that well-being comparisons played a significant role in mental health outcomes following loss, with aversive comparisons contributing uniquely to psychopathology. However, the study had limitations, including a relatively small sample size and potential underpowering for mediation effects. The findings suggest that interventions targeting maladaptive well-being comparisons may be beneficial for bereaved individuals. The study highlights the importance of understanding cognitive processes like well-being comparisons in the context of grief and mental health.


### Candidate B

This longitudinal study investigated whether aversive well-being comparisons—including social, temporal, and counterfactual comparisons—serve as a cognitive link between bereavement and psychopathology. Using a two-wave design, researchers assessed 315 bereaved individuals at baseline and six months later. Participants were evaluated on comparison frequency, perceived discrepancy, and affective impact, alongside measures of prolonged grief disorder, PTSD, depression, anxiety, somatic symptoms, and loneliness. Results indicated that while grief and distress symptoms significantly decreased over six months, comparison frequency significantly predicted anxiety, somatic symptoms, and loneliness at the second assessment. Cross-sectional analyses revealed that comparison frequency significantly predicted depression, anxiety, and somatic symptoms, with these relationships mediated by perceived discrepancy and negative affective impact. Specifically, the indirect effects for depression (0.13, $p = .036$), anxiety (0.16, $p = .030$), and somatic symptoms (0.54, $p = .036$) were significant. The study concludes that aversive comparisons are independent predictors of distress, potentially exacerbating loss-oriented grieving. However, the study was limited by a 28% dropout rate, reliance on self-reports, and being underpowered to detect longitudinal mediation effects.


## paper028

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper028.pdf)

### Candidate A

This cross-sectional study investigated latent classes of adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) and their associations with impulsivity and mental health among adults in Saudi Arabia. Using an online convenience sample of 379 adults aged 18–65, researchers employed latent class analysis (LCA) to identify patterns of adversity. Participants were categorized into a structural no-ACE reference group and two ACE-exposed classes: limited/mixed ACE and poly-victimisation. The poly-victimisation class, characterized by high probabilities of emotional abuse, physical abuse, and household mental illness, showed significantly higher levels of negative urgency (d = 0.55) and lack of perseverance (d = 0.43) compared to the no-ACE group. Furthermore, this class was associated with a higher prevalence of lifetime psychiatric diagnosis (OR = 6.27) and greater perceived health impact (OR = 12.81). The study concludes that a poly-victimisation pattern is associated with greater emotion-related impulsivity and mental health burden, supporting the use of pattern-level assessments alongside cumulative ACE counts. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design, which precludes causal inference, the use of self-reported data, and the reliance on a convenience sample and brief, single-item mental health measures.


### Candidate B

This study investigated latent classes of adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) and their associations with impulsivity and mental health among adults in Saudi Arabia. A cross-sectional online survey of 379 adults aged 18–65 years was conducted, using the 10-item ACE screener, the Arabic Short UPPS-P Impulsive Behaviour Scale, and single-item measures of lifetime psychiatric diagnosis and perceived health impact. A structural no-ACE reference group was retained, and latent class analysis (LCA) identified two classes among ACE-exposed participants: limited/mixed ACE (n = 140, 36.9%) and poly-victimisation (n = 106, 28.0%). The poly-victimisation class showed higher negative urgency (d = 0.55, q < .001), lack of perseverance (d = 0.43, q = .007), psychiatric diagnosis (OR = 6.27, 95% CI [3.19, 12.31]), and perceived health impact (OR = 12.81, 95% CI [6.91, 23.75]) compared to the no-ACE group. Family conflict interactions were exploratory and not statistically robust. The findings suggest that poly-victimisation is associated with greater emotion-related impulsivity and mental health burden. While LCA provides complementary pattern-level information, it should not replace cumulative ACE scores. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, brief outcome measures, and potential biases in self-reporting. The study highlights the importance of considering ACE patterns in clinical and public health contexts.


## paper039

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper039.pdf)

### Candidate A

This study evaluated a national, three-tiered knowledge mobilization initiative designed to improve support for UK women veterans who have experienced military sexual trauma (MST). Guided by the Knowledge-to-Action framework and co-produced with women veterans, the program aimed to enhance professional knowledge, improve service engagement strategies, and increase clinical capacity for Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT). The methodology employed a mixed-methods approach involving three components: a self-guided online foundational course for 526 individuals, a live online workshop for 363 professionals, and a truncated CPT training for 98 clinicians across 34 organizations. Quantitative analysis of the third course showed that clinician self-efficacy in delivering CPT significantly increased at a three-month follow-up ($p < .001, r = 0.915$), with a mean confidence increase of 1.00 (SE = 0.12). Qualitative findings highlighted themes regarding clinician confidence and systemic barriers to CPT delivery. The study concluded that the program successfully addressed knowledge gaps and demonstrated that co-produced knowledge mobilization can translate research into real-world impact. Limitations included low participation from Scotland, a potentially insufficient three-month follow-up period for assessing long-term systemic change, and the use of true-or-false quizzes for knowledge consolidation.


### Candidate B

This paper describes a three-part online education and training program designed to enhance professional knowledge and improve support for UK women veterans who have experienced military sexual trauma (MST). Guided by knowledge mobilization (KMb) theory and the Knowledge-to-Action (KTA) framework, the program aimed to address gaps in service provider understanding and increase clinical capacity for evidence-based treatments, particularly cognitive processing therapy (CPT). The study involved 684 participants, including healthcare professionals, veteran specialists, and policy makers, who engaged with three courses: an introductory course on women veterans’ experiences, an advanced course on service adaptations, and a CPT training course. Mixed-methods data were collected, with quantitative analysis showing a significant increase in self-efficacy in delivering CPT among participants three months post-training (p < .001, r = 0.915). Qualitative feedback highlighted clinicians’ confidence in CPT, perceptions of its suitability for different patient groups, and challenges in implementation due to resource limitations and lack of training. The study found that the program successfully enhanced professional knowledge and supported the development of evidence-based practices, though limitations include the need for ongoing monitoring of long-term impact and broader engagement beyond veteran-specific organizations. The initiative demonstrates how research can be translated into real-world impact for military populations, emphasizing the value of co-produced KMb approaches.


## paper047

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper047.pdf)

### Candidate A

This study is a population-based legacy analysis of myasthenia gravis (MG) in Iceland, aiming to assess its prevalence and incidence alongside clinical characteristics. Data were collected from 1981 to 2002 using hospital records, neurologists, immunology labs, and the national MG association, with inclusion requiring characteristic symptoms and at least one confirmatory test. Point prevalence was calculated on 31 December 2002, and incidence for 1997–2002. The study found a prevalence of 9.0 per 100,000 and a mean annual incidence of 0.78 per 100,000. The female-to-male ratio was 1.2:1, with a mean age at onset of 43 years. Ocular symptoms were predominant at presentation, and 19% experienced diagnostic delays exceeding three years. By 2003, 54% had generalized MG, and 27% were in complete remission. The study highlights the value of legacy datasets for rare disease surveillance, though it does not establish causality. Limitations include the small cohort size and potential underdiagnosis due to historical diagnostic practices. The findings align with global trends of increasing prevalence and incidence, suggesting improved detection and survival rates.


### Candidate B

This population-based legacy study investigated the epidemiology and clinical characteristics of myasthenia gravis (MG) in Iceland, a small, genetically homogeneous Arctic nation. Researchers analyzed a nationwide cohort of patients diagnosed between 1981 and 2002, utilizing centralized healthcare records and laboratory data. Inclusion required characteristic symptoms and at least one confirmatory test, such as positive acetylcholine receptor antibodies (AChRAb), positive response to acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, or abnormal electrophysiology. The study identified a point prevalence of 9.0 per 100,000 inhabitants as of December 2002, with an annual incidence of 0.78 per 100,000 from 1997 to 2002. The mean age at symptom onset was 43 years, with a female-to-male ratio of 1.2:1. Notably, 73% of patients initially presented with ocular symptoms, and 46% were asymptomatic or in remission by 2003. While the study observed an upward trend in prevalence compared to earlier historical data, the authors suggest this may reflect improved survival and diagnostic sensitivity rather than a true increase in disease incidence. Limitations include the small cohort size and the retrospective mapping of the Osserman staging system to contemporary MGFA classifications.


## paper082

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper082.pdf)

### Candidate A

The DISTAL study was a 12-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial investigating whether a potato fiber and sugar beet pectin mixture could improve metabolic health in adults with overweight/obesity and impaired glucose metabolism. Participants were assigned to receive either the fiber mixture or an isocaloric maltodextrin placebo while adhering to a high-protein, partially plant-based diet. The primary outcome was peripheral insulin sensitivity (IS) measured via hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp. Results indicated that the fiber group showed a trend toward lower peripheral insulin sensitivity compared to the placebo group (p=0.081), while whole-body IS significantly decreased in the fiber group (p=0.034). Furthermore, fiber supplementation significantly reduced insulin-mediated carbohydrate oxidation (p=0.027) and fat oxidation (p=0.006) compared to the placebo. Notably, the fiber group experienced increased colonic gut permeability (p=0.046) and elevated plasma interleukin-6 levels (p=0.025). The study concluded that the fiber supplement, when combined with a high-protein diet, may have hindered metabolic improvements and compromised gut barrier function. Limitations include the potential influence of the maltodextrin placebo and the inability to isolate the fiber's effects from the high-protein diet's effects.


### Candidate B

This study aimed to evaluate the effects of a slowly fermentable fiber mixture, composed of potato fiber and sugar beet pectin, on insulin sensitivity and metabolic health in individuals with overweight, when consumed alongside a high-protein diet. The 12-week randomized, placebo-controlled trial involved 44 participants, with 19 receiving the fiber mixture and 23 receiving maltodextrin as a placebo. The primary outcome was peripheral insulin sensitivity, measured using a two-step hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp. Results showed that peripheral insulin sensitivity tended to decrease in the fiber group compared to placebo (p = 0.081), while whole-body insulin sensitivity significantly decreased (p = 0.034). Fiber mitigated the increase in insulin-mediated carbohydrate oxidation and the decrease in fat oxidation observed in the placebo group. Additionally, fiber prevented an increase in protein oxidation and increased colonic gut permeability and plasma interleukin-6 levels. Body composition, microbial composition, and fecal and circulating metabolites remained unchanged. The study concluded that the fiber mixture reduced peripheral insulin sensitivity and metabolic flexibility compared to placebo, possibly due to reduced amino acid bioavailability and potential compromise of gut barrier function. However, no significant differences in microbial composition or functionality were observed between groups. The study highlights the complex interplay between dietary fibers and proteins, suggesting that further research is needed to understand their combined effects on metabolic health. Limitations include the use of a placebo that may have influenced host metabolism and the inability to disentangle the effects of the fiber mixture from those of the high-protein diet.


## paper083

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper083.pdf)

### Candidate A

This study aimed to investigate the clinical characteristics, pathology, and prognosis of IgA nephropathy (IgAN) and comorbid diabetic kidney disease (DKD). A single-centre retrospective study included 80 patients with biopsy-confirmed IgAN + DKD (2010–2023), and propensity score matching (PSM) was used to create comparable groups of IgAN and DKD. Baseline clinical and pathological data, treatments, and outcomes were analyzed. In the unmatched cohort, IgAN + DKD patients had higher baseline sCr and 24-hour urinary protein (24h-uPro) and lower microscopic haematuria (MH) than IgAN. After matching, differences in 24h-uPro and MH persisted. IgAN + DKD showed fewer crescents, lower KM55/IgA ratio, and weaker C3 deposition. Compared to DKD, IgAN + DKD had more MH but milder glomerular lesions and arteriolar hyalinosis. In patients with 1-year data, 24h-uPro of IgAN + DKD declined substantially, with 58.06% overall clinical remission. The median time free of the composite kidney endpoint was 65.63 months, with higher risk than IgAN but lower than DKD. Baseline sCr and irreversible GBM thickening were associated with endpoints. Unselected immunosuppression showed no long-term renal benefit. The study concluded that IgAN + DKD exhibited lower intrarenal immune activity than IgAN and less severe diabetic glomerular injury than DKD. The KM55/IgA ratio might aid differentiation from IgAN. Long-term renal prognosis was intermediate between the monodisease groups. Routine immunosuppression reduced short-term proteinuria but did not improve hard renal endpoints. Higher baseline sCr and GBM thickness linked to poorer outcomes. Key limitations include the retrospective, single-centre design, potential selection bias, and confounding by indication.


### Candidate B

This retrospective study aimed to characterize the clinical, pathological, and prognostic features of patients with biopsy-confirmed coexisting IgA nephropathy (IgAN) and diabetic kidney disease (DKD). Researchers analyzed 80 patients with IgAN + DKD between 2010 and 2023, using propensity score matching to establish comparable cohorts of 80 patients each with primary IgAN and DKD. Results indicated that IgAN + DKD patients exhibited higher baseline serum creatinine and 24-hour urinary protein compared to those with primary IgAN, while showing lower rates of microscopic hematuria than both other groups. Pathologically, IgAN + DKD demonstrated milder glomerular lesions than DKD but more severe tubulointerstitial damage than IgAN. The KM55/IgA ratio was significantly lower in the IgAN + DKD group, suggesting its utility as a biomarker to distinguish it from primary IgAN. Prognostically, the risk of renal deterioration in IgAN + DKD was significantly higher than in IgAN but lower than in DKD. Furthermore, unselected immunosuppressive therapy did not provide measurable long-term renal benefits. Limitations include the retrospective, single-center design, potential selection bias due to biopsy requirements, and high attrition rates in follow-up data.


## paper156

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper156.pdf)

### Candidate A

This study investigated whether biological sex influences natural killer (NK) cell-mediated immunity against pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). Using a syngeneic allograft model and *in vitro* co-cultures, researchers evaluated primary NK cells isolated from male and female C57BL/6JRj mice against the Panc02 cell line. Results demonstrated that female NK cells exhibited significantly higher cytotoxic activity, increasing the destruction of target cells by approximately 50% compared to male NK cells. *In vivo*, female mice showed slower tumor growth (37.4-day doubling time) and smaller final tumor volumes (67.6 mm³) compared to males (20.8-day doubling time; 196.0 mm³). The study found that female NK cells possess intrinsic epigenetic advantages, specifically linked to *Kdm6a* expression and H3K27me3 status, which were independent of acute sex hormones. Furthermore, the female tumor microenvironment was more immune-permissive, characterized by higher NK cell infiltration and pro-inflammatory signatures, whereas male tumors featured a denser, more immunosuppressive stroma. While these findings suggest sex-specific NK cell contributions to anti-tumor immunity, limitations include the use of a subcutaneous mouse model, lack of estrous cycle monitoring, and the need for NK-depletion studies to confirm causality.


### Candidate B

This study investigates the influence of biological sex on natural killer (NK) cell-mediated immunity against pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). Using in vitro and in vivo models, the researchers found that female-derived NK cells exhibit superior cytotoxic activity and immune activation compared to male-derived NK cells. These findings were corroborated in a syngeneic allograft PDAC model, where female mice showed enhanced NK cell function and distinct tumor microenvironment (TME) characteristics that supported stronger NK cell activity. The study highlights that host sex significantly shapes the TME composition and architecture, with female tumors displaying a more immune-permissive environment and reduced proliferation. Female NK cells showed increased expression of genes associated with immune response and cytotoxicity, such as granzyme B, and exhibited enhanced antitumor activity independent of sex hormones. However, the study does not establish causality or clinical outcomes, and the observed associations are not definitive. Limitations include the use of a single cell line and the lack of direct clinical validation. The findings suggest that sex differences in NK cell function may differentially impact tumor control in PDAC, emphasizing the importance of considering sex as a biological variable in preclinical research.


## paper157

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper157.pdf)

### Candidate A

This study investigated the mechanisms underlying ulcerative colitis (UC) and associated hepatobiliary complications, specifically focusing on the gut–liver axis and hepatic lipid metabolism. Researchers utilized mouse models (C57BL/6J and 129/Sv) and human metabolomic/transcriptomic datasets to evaluate the roles of pristimerin (PSM), the commensal *Lactobacillus johnsonii*, and the LXRα–SCD1 signaling pathway. Results demonstrated that DSS-induced colitis increased saturated free fatty acids and saturated lysophospholipids while decreasing unsaturated species. PSM and *L. johnsonii* culture supernatant (Ljsup) significantly reduced the Disease Activity Index (DAI) and systemic inflammatory markers, reversing these lipid trends. Specifically, Ljsup activated the hepatic LXRα–SCD1 axis, increasing anti-inflammatory lipid species like LPC20:2 and LPC20:3. Conversely, corticosterone (CORT) exacerbated UC and liver injury by downregulating LXRα signaling, an effect mitigated by the LXRα antagonist SR9243. Human data corroborated these findings, showing that elevated ratios of specific lysophospholipids and ceramides correlated with higher clinical activity scores. The study concludes that hepatic lipid dysregulation, driven by impaired LXRα–SCD1 signaling, contributes to UC comorbidity. Limitations include the use of animal models, a lack of human clinical trials, and potential sex-related hormonal influences.


### Candidate B

This study investigated the protective effects of pristimerin (PSM) against ulcerative colitis (UC) and associated liver injury, focusing on the role of the gut microbiota and the hepatic LXRα–SCD1 signaling pathway. The research used mouse models of UC induced by dextran sulfate sodium (DSS) and analyzed the impact of PSM on lipid metabolism, inflammation, and liver function. PSM was found to alleviate UC symptoms, reduce liver injury, and modulate lipid metabolism by activating the LXRα–SCD1 pathway. The gut commensal Lactobacillus johnsonii was identified as a mediator of PSM's protective effects, as it enhanced the expression of SCD1 and promoted the production of anti-inflammatory lipid species such as LPC20:2 and LPC20:3. The study also demonstrated that corticosterone (CORT) inhibited the LXRα–SCD1 pathway, exacerbating inflammation and liver damage. In human data, similar lipid metabolic alterations were observed in UC patients, with elevated ceramides and altered LPC/LPE ratios correlating with disease severity. The findings suggest that targeting the LXRα–SCD1 pathway and modulating gut microbiota could be promising therapeutic strategies for UC and its associated liver complications. However, the study did not establish causality, and further research is needed to validate these mechanisms in clinical settings. Limitations include reliance on animal models and the use of public datasets for human data, which may not fully represent the complexity of human disease.


## paper193

[Open original PDF](../inputs/pdfs/paper193.pdf)

### Candidate A

This qualitative study explored the perspectives of pregnant women and their families regarding the centralization of maternity services in Kalaallit Nunaat (Greenland). Utilizing the Peqqissuserput model of Inuit health, researchers conducted semi-structured interviews with 20 participants, including 14 pregnant women, one expecting father, and five elderly community members across five health regions. The study aimed to understand how centralized delivery services—requiring travel to regional hospitals—impact the cultural, emotional, and social dimensions of childbirth. Findings identified four primary themes: practicalities and birthing, being together or alone, family formation, and identity and sense of belonging. Results indicated that while local births foster emotional security, relational continuity, and community connection, centralized services often lead to feelings of loneliness, powerlessness, and anxiety due to separation from support networks. Participants expressed concerns that relocating births disrupts Inuit culture, diminishes the woman’s central role, and potentially weakens the newborn's connection to their roots. The study concludes that childbirth is a deeply relational and cultural process; therefore, healthcare should adopt culturally grounded, community-based models that prioritize autonomy and intergenerational bonds. A limitation is the exclusion of women who experienced complicated births.


### Candidate B

Using the Peqqissuserput model, which emphasizes relational and cultural dimensions of health, the research identifies four key themes: practicalities and birthing, being together or alone, family formation, and identity and sense of belonging. Findings reveal that centralization disrupts traditional Inuit birthing practices, leading to emotional distress, loss of shared family memories, and weakened community ties. In contrast, local births foster emotional support, cultural continuity, and a stronger sense of belonging. The research underscores the importance of considering cultural and community contexts in maternity care, emphasizing the need for policies that respect Inuit values and support local birthing options.
