This study examined whether Moringa oleifera leaf extract could protect against cuprizone-induced demyelination in rats. Twenty-four male Wistar rats were assigned to control, extract-only, cuprizone-only, or combined-treatment groups for three weeks; the extract dose was 300 mg/kg/day. Co-administration improved motor coordination and grip performance, reduced oxidative stress and inflammatory markers in the corpus callosum, and improved myelin and oligodendrocyte structure relative to cuprizone alone. Chemical profiling identified extract constituents, while molecular docking suggested potential interactions with NF-kappaB and TNF-alpha; computational pharmacokinetic analyses further explored their drug-like properties. Together, the findings support a potential protective effect involving antioxidant and anti-inflammatory processes. However, NF-kappaB modulation remains a proposed mechanism, with confirmatory molecular analyses lacking. The experiment assessed concurrent protection in a small animal model, rather than treatment of established human demyelinating disease, so therapeutic applicability requires further investigation.
