This study evaluated the diagnostic accuracy of GPT-5-Thinking, a large language model, in interpreting MRI reports of orbital and head-and-neck tumors. The research aimed to determine whether GPT-5-Thinking could bridge the diagnostic gap between generalist and subspecialist radiologists. In a dual-center retrospective study involving 1000 patients, GPT-5-Thinking demonstrated top-1 accuracy similar to subspecialists for orbital tumors (76.7% vs 78.3%) and head-and-neck tumors (67.5% vs 68.0%). It also showed higher accuracy than routine generalists for both tumor types. GPT-5-Thinking assistance improved generalist diagnostic accuracy for orbital tumors from 61.4% to 70.3% and for head-and-neck tumors from 47.0% to 61.0%. However, the study had limitations, including non-consecutive sampling and potential biases due to the reliance on existing MRI reports. These findings suggest that GPT-5-Thinking may support generalists in achieving more accurate diagnoses, but further prospective studies are needed to confirm its effectiveness in clinical practice.
