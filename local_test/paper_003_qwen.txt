This study investigated the neuroprotective effects of Moringa oleifera leaf extract (MOLE) against cuprizone (CPZ)-induced demyelination in rats. MOLE was found to significantly improve neurobehavioral deficits, oxidative stress, and inflammatory markers in the corpus callosum (CC) of CPZ-treated rats. Biochemical analyses showed that MOLE reduced oxidative stress markers such as malondialdehyde (MDA) and nitric oxide (NO), while increasing antioxidant levels like glutathione (GSH), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and catalase (CAT). Inflammatory cytokines, including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and NF-κB, were also reduced in MOLE-treated rats. Histological and ultrastructural assessments revealed improved myelination and reduced demyelination in the CC of rats co-treated with MOLE and CPZ. Molecular docking studies indicated that MOLE's bioactive compounds interact with NF-κB and TNF-α, supporting their role in modulating inflammatory pathways. In silico pharmacokinetic analysis suggested favorable absorption and BBB penetration of MOLE's compounds, with no significant toxicity. These findings suggest that MOLE may offer a promising natural therapeutic approach for demyelinating disorders by mitigating oxidative stress and inflammation. However, the study did not include molecular mechanisms or long-term effects, and further research is needed to confirm these results.
