This phase 2, randomized, active-controlled, observer-blind study evaluated the safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity of two investigational mRNA vaccines against SARS-CoV-2, compared to a licensed bivalent mRNA vaccine. The study included 425 healthy adults aged ≥18 years who had completed a primary vaccination series with or without a booster at least three months prior. Participants were randomized to receive either a bivalent mRNA vaccine (CV0701) encoding ancestral D614G and Omicron BA.4–5 spike proteins at three dose levels, a monovalent mRNA vaccine (CV0601) encoding Omicron BA.4–5 spike protein, or a licensed comparator vaccine. The primary endpoints were reactogenicity, safety, and immunogenicity, with secondary and tertiary endpoints focusing on humoral and cell-mediated immune responses. Most solicited events were mild to moderate, and no vaccine-related serious adverse events or cases of myocarditis/pericarditis were reported. For the CV0701 vaccine, neutralizing titers against both ancestral D614G and Omicron BA.4–5 increased dose-dependently at Day 29 and remained above baseline at Day 181. The CV0601 vaccine showed similar immune responses. CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell responses were observed in all groups, with the lowest dose of CV0701 showing a lesser response. The investigational vaccines demonstrated an acceptable safety profile and robust immunogenicity, comparable to the licensed comparator vaccine. The study limitations include the small sample size and lack of formal hypothesis testing, which may limit the ability to detect rare events or draw definitive comparative conclusions. These findings support the potential of these vaccines in addressing the evolving SARS-CoV-2 variants.
