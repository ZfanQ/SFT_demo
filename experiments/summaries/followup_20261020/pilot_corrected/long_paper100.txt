This study investigated the protective effects of hirsutine, an indole alkaloid, against high glucose (HG)-induced injury in MPC5 podocytes, an in vitro model of podocyte dysfunction in diabetic nephropathy (DN). MPC5 cells were exposed to 30 mM HG with or without hirsutine (5–20 μM), and cell viability, oxidative stress, apoptosis, and ferroptosis were assessed. Hirsutine significantly improved cell viability, reduced intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, and suppressed apoptosis, indicating its cytoprotective role. It also modulated autophagy by altering LC3 and p62 expression and inhibited ferroptosis by upregulating NRF2 pathway markers and reducing lipid peroxidation. These findings suggest that hirsutine exerts protective effects through the coordinated regulation of apoptosis, autophagy, and ferroptosis. However, the study is limited to an in vitro model and lacks direct autophagic flux assessment and genetic validation of NRF2 dependency. The results highlight hirsutine's potential as a therapeutic candidate for DN, but further in vivo studies are needed to confirm its efficacy and mechanism of action.
