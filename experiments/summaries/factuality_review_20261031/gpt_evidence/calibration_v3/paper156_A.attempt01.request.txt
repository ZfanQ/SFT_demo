Female tumors grow more slowly, which is\ncorrelated with a higher NK cell infiltration. Following up from here, multiplex\nspatial immunofluorescence staining and RNA-seq revealed differences in the overall tumor composition between males and females. In addition, their strong sexdependent regulation might be far more relevant in the context of naturally arising tumors than in\nthe high-dose allograft injection model, as they are crucial for the control of single transformed cells\nand metastases.2 The data from this study support the conclusion that NK cells provide a sex-specific\ncontribution to anti-tumor immunity in concert with the TME and other immune cells. Future studies\ncombining single-cell and spatial transcriptomic approaches may therefore be needed to more precisely\ndefine sex-specific immune programs in the tumor microenvironment.
